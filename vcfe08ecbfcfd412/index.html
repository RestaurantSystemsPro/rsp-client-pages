Kitchen production, the number that drives the schedule

METHOD
A good, efficient kitchen produces about $100 of food per labor hour. The target set for this store is $71.34.
That target is deliberately generous, and here is exactly where it comes from, so it can be checked line by line rather than taken on trust.
Kitchen and dish budget for next week$4,046.48 over 233.06 hours
Average back of house wage$17.36 per hour
That budget as a share of the $28,557.13 sales plan14.17%
$17.36 divided by 14.17% gives total sales needed per labor hour$122.53
This store's food mix, because 40% of sales are liquor58.22%
$122.53 times 58.22% gives the food production target$71.34 of food per back of house hour
Next week as currently scheduled, 290.50 hours$57.23 per hour
At the sales run rate of the last two weeks$52.35 per hour
Hours that would hit the $71.34 target233 hours, so cut 57 to 77
Why this store's number looks low, and why that is not an excuse. Most restaurants run near 80% food. Vivio's runs about 40% liquor, so out of a $5,000 night only about $2,900 is food for the kitchen to produce. A bartender is simply more productive per hour than a cook, because pouring a glass of wine takes a fraction of the labor that building a salad does. That is why the target was set at $71.34 instead of $100. It also means the restaurant can feel genuinely busy while the kitchen does not have enough food volume to justify the hours on the board. The bar mix explains the target. It does not explain a $37 per hour shift.
The shifts that stand out. Tuesday next week produces about $37 per labor hour, which is barely double the wage being paid to produce it. Next Saturday produces about $91 per labor hour and sits nearly 11 hours under budget, which is what a well built day looks like and what buys back room elsewhere. The question worth asking at the schedule meeting is simple: why does Tuesday carry 36 hours when Saturday carries 38 on far higher sales, and why does Friday carry as many hours as it does when the forecast for Friday is not materially higher. Sunday and Friday both look heavy. Monday and Sunday carry real upside with football, so they are the two days worth protecting rather than trimming.

Cash trail, last 12 nights

How to read this. Expected cash is arithmetic, not opinion: the full amount due for the day less every card, delivery and in kind tender, less anything paid out of the drawer. When a counted deposit is recorded, the over or short column is a real till result. When no count is recorded, the over or short figure is simply the whole expected deposit and means nothing about the drawer.

The record here is good. Nine of twelve nights counted, and five of those landed within one dollar of expectation. Saturday 09-05 is the single genuine variance in the run, $78.96 short on a $697.96 expectation. That one is worth a look at who counted and whether a paid out went unrecorded, because it is the only night in twelve that does not prove. Three nights remain open and are covered above.

Cost of goods, week of 08-31 to 09-06

CategorySalesCostCost %Target %Variance $
Food is the headline, and it is good news. Food cost came in at 23.87% against a 27.00% target, $488.28 favourable. That matters for two reasons. It is money, and it is proof: a genuine counting problem inflates food along with everything else, and food went the other way. The count is sound.

Which isolates the problem to beverage, and makes it much easier to fix. Read as one group, beverage cost $4,081.68 on $10,492.72 of beverage sales, 38.90% against a 20.04% blended target, $1,979.06 over. Do not read the individual lines as performance: bottle beer printing 84.71% and non alcoholic beverage printing 220.20% are not purchasing results, and wine printing a negative 36.94% is arithmetically impossible on a clean count. Those three together say beverage purchases are landing in a neighbouring category, or in cost without landing in a count.
And here is the likely mechanism, with dates. Twenty one new beverage products were set up in the last thirty days: twelve bar items, including Founders Oktoberfest quarter barrels, Griffin Claw Screamin Pumpkin, Down East Cider Blackberry, Garage Beer Light, Shorts Local Honey Crisp, Furlan Prosecco and Four Roses, plus nine non alcoholic items, which is five canned soft drinks, two waters, a caramel syrup and a creamer. A product that has not been given an inventory location is never counted, so its entire purchase reads as consumed that week. Give every one of those twenty one a location and a category before the next count and the beverage lines should move most of the way back on their own. The prize is $1,979.06 a week, roughly $103,000 a year at this run rate.
